
Pain points:
1. Toast has no concept of quantities on different days
	- This only makes sense when orders are just placed in the restaurant for food immediately i.e. a sit down restaurant or a small retail store
	- For a quick serve restaurant that accepts preorders with items that are made daily, this does not really function well and it is difficult to prevent both over and underselling at the same time
	- If you take the cautious approach and limit stock quantities of items to prevent overselling today, you will often undersell, and prevent preorders for future days that you could otherwise fulfill
	- If you are liberal with counts to allow for preorders, you will risk overselling for today and lose customer goodwill
2. Items with shared ingredients cannot be put on a shared limited stock
	- For example, sandwiches that all use turkey cannot be 86d due to turkey selling out, and they all have different counts so there is no way to prevent overselling other than 86ing all items that share that ingredient and only selling them for dine in orders
3. Different ordering channels have different inventories
	- Items sold on both the regular online/POS menu and catering do not share inventories, making counts not able to limit sales unless you allocate a split amount between both channels, but this requires supervision to avoid underselling because of a sellout on one channel and not the other
4. Fractional inventory quantities when not needed
	- This is a small one, but the fact that the quantities automatically turn into floating point numbers just adds unnecessary key presses to update them in most cases. In the case of adding a quantity in a busy service, time is really of the essence and this adds friction

Wishlist
1. Ability to set the stocks of items in the future
2. Allow preorders in the future when an item is out of stock today (when applicable)
